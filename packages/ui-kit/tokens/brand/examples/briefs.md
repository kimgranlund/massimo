# Briefs for the worked brands

The briefs each worked brand was authored from ([author a brand](../../../../../docs/guides/author-a-brand.md)). Use them as a reproduction check: an agent given a brief and the guide should arrive at a brand whose character reads as the worked one (the Brand page's Compare table) and whose signature matches.

## Fjord Ledger (`fjord-ledger.json`)

A bookkeeping tool for small Nordic shipping firms. Bookkeepers reconcile a month of entries at a time and must never doubt a number. They work early mornings and late evenings in harbor offices. Think tide tables and ledger ruling, not maritime decoration. The team loves classic Swiss graphic design and wants it straight, not reinterpreted: the grid, objective type, no ornament. Their world is the working harbor they look out on. The one thing that must stand out is how much fits on a screen: a whole month, dense but calm, without scrolling.

## Kiln & Cup (`kiln-and-cup.json`)

An online shop for a ceramics studio that sells wood-fired tableware. Each piece is one of a kind. Buyers need to trust how a piece will feel in the hand from photographs alone. The studio admires Lucie Rie and the Japanese folk-craft tradition (mingei), but it is not a Japanese brand and does not want brush lettering, so take the tradition's values, not its look. The site should feel like the studio itself, the workshop after a firing. The one detail to get right is the rim of a thrown pot: every corner should have that continuous, never-drafted curve. The tone is warm and plain, with no hard sell.

## Night Signal (`night-signal.json`)

The companion site for a weekend electronic music festival. People use it on a phone, in the dark, in a crowd, to find who plays where right now. It should carry the energy of the stages without becoming the confetti-and-squiggles look of so many festival brands (Memphis is exactly what they don't want). The organizers want the Bauhaus stage workshop's construction and primary forms, reinterpreted for night. The world is the city after dark. They love Sonia Delaunay's color rhythms: the relation between the stage colors is what should be unmistakable.
