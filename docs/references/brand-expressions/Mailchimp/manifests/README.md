# Mailchimp — COLLINS case study

[Open the offline gallery](../index.html).

## Source and scope

Collected 2026-11-05 from [COLLINS’ Mailchimp case study](https://wearecollins.com/case-studies/mailchimp/). This collection preserves the complete distinct artwork and media sequence exposed by that case-study payload: one cover, five “Augments” boards and 21 gallery media records. It is a case study, not a complete brand book. The four photographed guideline spreads label themselves Version 1.0; the case-study body does not supply a definitive edition date for the whole collection. The live editorial narrative includes later business developments, so its current retrieval date must not be treated as the identity’s design date.

COLLINS credits its team, Mailchimp Brand Team and R/GA. Source rationale is attributed in individual purpose evidence. Strategic benefits in our analysis are interpretations; the agency’s commercial impact claims are not treated as independent causal evidence.

## Coverage and source handling

- 30 PNG/manifest pairs: 26 distinct case-study artworks or sequences plus four separately readable guideline spread frames from CH25.
- Ten local playable clips: eight GIF playback derivatives and two complete public Mux HLS streams remuxed to MP4. Original GIFs remain preserved. GIF derivatives use H.264/yuv420p, with a one-pixel pad where needed for even dimensions; the PNGs retain the complete native source image.
- CH17 is deliberately absent: gallery item 11 repeats the same poster photograph with a slightly different crop, already preserved as CH05 at 12500 × 8333. Its original lower-resolution JPEG remains in sources. No other source entries were removed. A diagram with annotations, an unannotated animated version and a separate application remain distinct lessons.
- CH25A–D preserve all four complete photographed spreads, in the GIF’s order. They are excerpt panels, not a claim that the full book has been obtained. Printed left folios are 72, 52, 86 and 64 respectively. The physical pages beyond these spreads are not provided by this case study.
- All five augment boards retain the original 12500 × 8333 source resolution. Other raster sources retain their published original dimensions. CH01 renders the original 960 × 1200 SVG at its declared size.
- Each image was visually inspected; all ten clips were inspected at multiple positions across their full durations. Audio has not been analyzed and no sonic-brand claims are made.
- No case-study media was unavailable. Direct Mux `high.mp4` URLs returned 404; the public HLS sources were accessible and supplied both complete films.
- The original page HTML and serialized source payload are preserved alongside a decoded record. These are provenance records, not standalone offline replicas of the agency website.

## Important interpretation limits

CH25B preserves the source’s distinct digital/print and vertical/horizontal logo minimums without inventing a width or height axis. Its referenced small-scale toolkit is not included. CH25C flags apparently swapped specimen captions rather than silently correcting the historical artwork; the referenced settings table is not supplied. CH25D distinguishes the printed Cavendish color values from slightly different yellow pixels in other delivered assets. No palette usage percentages are inferred from equal swatch areas.

## Rebuild

From `brand-expressions`, run `python3 build-gallery.py Mailchimp Mailchimp`. The gallery embeds the canonical JSON content and uses document-relative links. It requires no server or network. Retain the entire Mailchimp folder when relocating it.

## Offline playback verification

The gallery uses `preload="none"` for video. Firefox could stall CH18 on initial playback with `preload="metadata"`, while the same original MP4 played after an explicit source reload. A constant-frame-rate re-encoding initially avoided the symptom, but it did not establish an encoding defect. With the shared gallery’s preload fix, the original variable-rate derivative plays normally and has been restored. This isolates the reproduced failure to Firefox’s metadata-preload path for this asset, rather than corrupt source frames.

All ten clips advance through ordinary `play()` calls in the relocated offline Firefox gallery, without a forced source reload or `load()` workaround. The original GIF, selected PNG and manifest remain unchanged.
