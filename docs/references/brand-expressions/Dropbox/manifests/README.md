# Dropbox — COLLINS case study

Source: [COLLINS / Dropbox](https://wearecollins.com/case-studies/dropbox/). Retrieved 2026-11-05. Historical identity launched in 2017 (the source links contemporaneous launch coverage); case-study prose and annotations may be retrospective. This is the complete distinct visual sequence of this case study, not a complete brand book. Three photographed guideline excerpts do not establish possession of the rest of that book.

## Coverage and provenance

- 27 PNG/JSON pairs: 19 still artworks and 8 complete locally playable clips. All 28 source media records were retrieved. No missing case-study media.
- DB02 is deliberately omitted: the cover clip is a differently framed rendition of the portrait logo animation retained at DB10. Its original MP4 remains under sources/. DB04 is retained because its landscape arrangement and color sequence differ.
- Annotated boards DB05/DB06 and gallery views DB22/DB27 intentionally coexist: the boards add comparisons or annotations rather than merely increasing image resolution.
- Original stills retain published native dimensions, including five 12500×8333 boards. PNG conversion preserves decoded RGB pixels. No artwork was reconstructed or upscaled.
- Mux HLS streams were remuxed without video transcoding into local MP4 files. Audio tracks, where supplied, are retained; analysis describes visual content and makes no sonic identity claims. Each clip was inspected at 12 timestamps across its duration.
- Saved case-study HTML, Nuxt payload and decoded source record preserve captions, credits and source context. Generic site chrome, press thumbnails and related-project recommendations are excluded from the project-artwork inventory.
- The case study makes business-impact claims, but these manifests do not infer that the identity caused those outcomes. No editable asset or current brand policy is implied.
