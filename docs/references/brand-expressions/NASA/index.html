V01  /  Color

Motor Vehicles: Various Colors & Values

Slide intent
Teach designers to read the lightness of a painted surface before choosing a mark color. People looking at a fleet vehicle need to distinguish its identification from the door behind it.
Strategic value
NASA can use one identification across cars acquired in different colors. Changing the lettering from black to white preserves the form of the mark while giving it contrast against a darker body.
Demonstration
Two side views hold the sedan, marking position and flush-left arrangement steady. Figure a uses black on the light car. Figure b uses white on the dark car. The text uses a 40% gray-scale value as the dividing line.
Category
Color
Practical value
Compare the body color with the manual’s gray scale. For this black-or-white treatment, use black markings at 40% or lighter and white markings above 40%. The adjacent section 7.3 gives white vehicles a separate red-logo option.
Content islands
  1. Heading and decision rule Place the title at upper left and run the explanation across two columns to its right. State the cutoff and keep the note about clearing door moldings with the rule.
  2. Matched sedan comparison Below a horizontal rule, stack two equally scaled side elevations in the same orientation. Label them a and b. Keep the lockup on the front door and carry its left alignment across both examples. Change the body value and lettering color together so the contrast decision is visible.

V02  /  Layout

The Identification Configuration With the Logotype

Slide intent
Teach designers to preserve a reading order when lettering must fit a real object. A handle, molding or door edge can interrupt a name that reads clearly on a flat drawing.
Strategic value
NASA can identify both the agency and the local center with the same arrangement on different vehicles. A common left edge helps the four pieces read as one identification even when the door hardware requires a shift.
Demonstration
The sheet pairs a small passenger-door placement with a large annotated elevation. The large diagram stacks the government-use lines, red NASA logotype, two-line agency name and research center. Inch dimensions establish a hierarchy within the group.
Category
Layout
Practical value
Use the annotated elevation to set the heights and spacing of the elements, then use the smaller placement example to place the group on the passenger door while keeping its elements flush left.
Content islands
  1. Heading and installation notes Place the heading in the first column and the installation notes in the next two. Explain that the center name may occupy one or two lines and that its position may need to shift around trim.
  2. Passenger-door inset Below the divider, set a small elevation at left. Keep the lockup and door outline together and identify this as the passenger-door example.
  3. Dimensioned door elevation Give the larger door drawing the lower-right field. Draw the outline, window, mirror and handle. Stack the four elements on a common left edge and flank them with dimension marks. Use a red logotype and black type. Show the center name 2 inches below the agency name as specified in the notes.

V03  /  Application

Motor Vehicle Identification Demonstrations

Slide intent
Teach designers which relationships to preserve when the carrier changes shape and scale. A person viewing a moving fleet encounters different silhouettes and viewing distances, yet still needs to identify the agency.
Strategic value
NASA can give a pickup, panel van or bus an identification suited to its body. Consistent door lettering can tie the vehicles together while a second, larger display of the logotype takes advantage of open panel space.
Demonstration
Seven labeled side elevations pair installation notes with a pickup, two panel trucks, a tractor-trailer van, a bus, a truck and a station wagon. The examples change the size, position and color of the logotype. The large white-and-blue vehicles carry the red mark at different scales.
Category
Application
Practical value
Choose the closest vehicle body and use the example to decide where lettering can sit flush left, whether the logotype can move away from the door and how large it can become.
Content islands
  1. Explanation and keyed notes Set the landscape sheet’s heading at upper left. Run the introduction and lettered installation notes across its upper band. Use a horizontal rule to separate text from drawings.
  2. Pickup and panel trucks Arrange a–c across the first drawing row. Compare the rear black mark on the light pickup with white marks placed on dark panels. Keep the label for each elevation beside its drawing.
  3. Large vehicles Place a broadside tractor-trailer van and a bus side by side in the middle row. Divide each body into white above and blue below, and use a red logotype on the white field. Associate the notes specifying a 24-inch logotype on the trailer and a 12-inch logotype on the bus.
  4. Truck and station wagon Place the white-cab, gray-body truck and white station wagon along the foot of the sheet. The truck carries the red logotype on its cab door. The station wagon separates its rear logotype from the lettering on the front door.
