The NASA Logotype: Use of Color

Slide intent
Teach designers to judge the mark against its actual background. A change in background value can change which lettering a viewer can distinguish.
Strategic value
NASA can keep a recognizable mark across one-color and multicolor applications by changing the foreground to suit the ground.
Demonstration
Three signatures compare red, black and warm gray on white. A white logotype on black sits below. The lowest specimen is partly obscured in the scan, while the text supplies the medium-value background rule.
Category
Color
Practical value
Use the background-value guidance to choose black, white or an approved color treatment before placing the master.
Reference purpose
  • Expression The visible specimens compare red, black and warm-gray signatures and white marks on dark grounds.
  • Application The text assigns logotype colors to white, very light, very dark and medium-value backgrounds.
  • Constraint It requires white on black, very dark or NASA-red grounds, and prohibits red on medium or dark grounds; red on very light grounds has a four-color-process exception.
Content islands
  1. Background guidance Introduce the choices for white, very light, dark and medium-value grounds in columns across the top.
  2. White-ground alternatives Arrange three signatures on white and hold their proportions and alignment steady as the mark changes color.
  3. Dark and middle-value specimens Place the white-on-black specimen below the three white-ground signatures. Retain the partly obscured lowest specimen as scanned; use the accompanying text to identify the medium-value rule.
Brand ingredient
Logotype foreground color
Role
Keep the logotype distinct from its background while preserving approved color treatments.
Application
On white, use the illustrated red-and-black, black or warm-gray treatment. On very light backgrounds, use black, except that red is allowed in very light areas of four-color reproduction. Use white on black, very dark or NASA-red grounds. On medium-value grounds, choose black or white, never red.
Scope
Background cases in the 1976 manual’s section 1.4. These are historical reproduction rules; the page gives no measured accessibility ratio.

Certificates & Awards

Slide intent
Teach designers to select the seal or logotype according to the significance of an award. The form of recognition sets an expectation for ceremony and permanence.
Strategic value
NASA can distinguish sustained service from shorter-term merit through both the identifying emblem and the arrangement of the certificate.
Demonstration
A traditional certificate centers the seal over the recipient’s name and signatures. Two smaller merit certificates use red logotypes with more contemporary type arrangements.
Category
Application
Practical value

NASA Vinylcals and Decals

Slide intent
Teach designers to specify an identification graphic together with its ground and measurement reference. The width of a label and the width of the printed mark describe two different products.
Strategic value
NASA can maintain legible markings on varied equipment and doors by matching the material and background to the surface.
Demonstration
A rounded white vinylcal carries a red logo and black agency name. A separate decal diagram presents the logo alone. Dimension lines span the vinylcal’s white field in one example and the mark’s baseline width in the other.
Category
Application
Practical value
Choose the white-field label for general surfaces and the freestanding decal for a transparent surface. Specify which dimension is being measured before ordering.
Content islands

  2. Vinylcal specification Pair the upper specimen with its field-width dimension and size notes. Preserve the rounded white ground and stacked agency name.
  3. Decal specification Place the isolated mark beneath the second divider with its baseline measurement. Keep the color, clearance and size instructions beside it.

NASA Uniform Patches

Slide intent
Teach designers to make an agency and a person’s role readable on clothing. Position and contrast must work with garment construction and with other badges a wearer may carry.
Strategic value
NASA can distinguish general personnel from emergency or security teams while retaining agency identification across uniforms, protective clothing and headwear.
Demonstration
Two patch specimens compare the general white patch with a red emergency or security patch and a separate dark service strip. Seven garment and equipment drawings show chest, shoulder, coat-back and helmet placements.
Category
Application
Practical value
Choose the patch by personnel role, then use the corresponding garment drawing to locate it. The lab coat and hardhat examples specify different sizes for their larger marks.
Content islands
  1. Personnel and placement guidance Set the title and explanatory columns along the top. Describe placement relative to a pocket and the special arrangement for emergency or security personnel.
  2. Patch specimens Place both systems side by side at upper right. Keep the service strip visibly separate beneath the red installation patch.
  3. Garment applications Arrange the flight jacket, shirt, flight suit, coat, blazer, emergency shirt and helmet in a single comparison field below the rule. Show each mark on the relevant visible surface.
