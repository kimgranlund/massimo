# MoMA — 2009 identity case study

Source: [Pentagram’s MoMA case study](https://www.pentagram.com/work/moma/story), retrieved 2026-11-05. This collection preserves all **8 distinct project artworks** in page order. It is an agency case study, not a complete brand standards book. No project video or downloadable full guide appears in the retrieved page. Navigation, next-project and news thumbnails are excluded.

Pentagram dates the identity refresh to 2009 and credits the pictured applications to Julia Hoffmann and MoMA’s in-house team. These references document that historical system, not current mandatory standards. Individual exhibitions may have their own identities; institutional examples here must not be generalized into rules for every exhibition.

## Source preservation and limits

The retrieved [HTML](../sources/pentagram-moma.html) retains accompanying explanation and captions. [Source inventory](../sources/inventory.json) records each signed published media URL, original asset name and downloaded pixel dimensions. The eight downloaded JPEG renditions are preserved without modification; each PNG is a lossless RGB conversion at the downloaded dimensions. Responsive repetitions and blurred placeholders are excluded. The hero is a publisher-selected crop (1023 × 638); no uncropped version of that hero is exposed by this page. Other images retain complete published artworks, up to 2000 pixels on their long edge. No artificial enlargement or reconstructed content is used. Small photographic text is not treated as a source for numerical rules.

The six-poster board remains one reference because its arrangement teaches comparison; its panels are not independently published assets. No animation is inferred from static sequences.
