## Source and scope

The collection preserves the complete distinct project-media sequence of [Pentagram’s Channel 4 case study](https://www.pentagram.com/work/channel-4), retrieved 5 November 2026: **62 references, including 29 playable clips and 33 still artworks**. This is a historical masterbrand case study associated with the 2023 rollout, not a complete downloadable guidelines book. The source includes useful construction and behavior demonstrations as well as applications. Pentagram credits its collaboration with 4creative and the specialist production collaborators in the retained page. [Channel 4’s announcement of 2 November 2022](https://www.channel4.com/press/news/channel-4-transforms-brand-help-viewers-navigate-crowded-digital-world) dates the transformation to spring 2023; individual examples, such as the 2024 Trends presentation, can depict later content.

C400 is the separate opening film. C401–C461 follow all 61 project-body figures in HTML order. The opening film, complete 147-second presentation film and extended closing loop remain separate because their duration and presentation differ. Repeated portions inside those sequences remain intact. Responsive renditions of the same artwork are deduplicated; the complete distinct figure remains. Website navigation, related-project promotions, news thumbnails and metadata-only social preview are excluded.

[Retained source HTML](../sources/case-study.html) contains the original captions, explanatory prose and media associations. [Source inventory](../sources/inventory.json) records every body figure’s signed published image URL, Vimeo ID, dimensions and duration; [opening film inventory](../sources/hero-inventory.json) records the separate hero. Each direct still link below is the actual retrieved signed lightbox rendition, not an invented raw-image URL. Landscape artwork is generally published at 2000 pixels wide; portrait artwork at 1886 pixels, with smaller source-specific exceptions. These are published JPEG derivatives of the site’s underlying PNGs. The paired PNGs preserve their decoded pixels exactly; they do not recreate unavailable original-resolution artwork.

## Motion and inspection

All 29 public Vimeo clips were retrieved from their publicly exposed player configurations and remuxed to local MP4 without re-encoding the video. No authentication or access restriction was bypassed. Original published poster artwork is retained for each body video, with the playable clip next to its manifest. C400 uses a frame extracted at 1 second because the opening embed has no project-body poster.

The poster and playable file sometimes represent different moments or alternate mockup environments. This is explicitly described where material: C413’s tabletop poster versus plain-surface phone video, C427’s ocean poster versus embrace footage, C437’s outdoor phone poster versus plain-surface video, and C438’s S-curve poster versus the video’s elasticity curve. Do not treat those poster differences as evidence of missing video frames.

Every still and every clip was visually inspected; short clips were inspected at 12 positions spanning their duration, and the 147-second film at 30 positions. This documents visual behavior, not a frame-by-frame transcription. Audio is retained where present but not analyzed or scored. No audio behavior is asserted. [Media validation](../sources/media-validation.json) records full-duration decode checks and local video metadata.

There are no missing project-media files in this captured sequence. The collection makes no claim to contain Channel 4’s full internal guidelines, production motion tools or font files. Published examples and stated principles are distinguished from universal requirements. Inferred benefits use “can”; Intent purposes cite the source’s own rationale. No color percentages, camera dimensions, font-size rules or universal easing values are invented from the examples.
